# Design Document — BONO: The Water That Connected Us

## 1. Project Intent

BONO tells the story of a communal drinking-water source that once served as an important part of everyday village life in Ethiopia.

The project is not simply about a water tap. It is about a shared place where people collected drinking water, interacted, and formed part of a community routine.

The documentary aims to preserve the memory and emotional significance of a place that no longer exists.

## 2. Intended Audience

The primary audience is people familiar with Ethiopian village life, particularly those who remember communal water sources and the routines surrounding them.

The film is also intended for younger viewers who may not have experienced this kind of communal infrastructure.

The intended emotional response is reflection, recognition, and appreciation of ordinary places that become meaningful through shared human experiences.

## 3. Narrative Approach

The film follows a simple progression:

1. Introduce BONO as a familiar communal water source.
2. Show how villagers used it to collect drinking water.
3. Emphasize the social interactions and routines around the source.
4. Reflect on its disappearance.
5. Conclude by emphasizing the connection between water, memory, and community.

The narration is in Amharic to preserve the cultural context and emotional character of the story. English subtitles make the film accessible to a wider audience.

## 4. Visual Direction

The visual approach is reflective and documentary-inspired.

Important visual elements include:

- The large silver water tap.
- The concrete structure surrounding the source.
- Yellow plastic jerrycans.
- Villagers collecting drinking water.
- The shared environment and everyday interactions.

The film uses faceless or non-identifiable AI-generated figures rather than claiming to depict actual historical residents.

The visual style aims for consistency across shots while maintaining a sense of place and everyday life.

## 5. Format and Editing Decisions

The production targets both vertical 9:16 and horizontal 16:9 formats.

The storyboard defines individual shots, their durations, visual prompts, motion prompts, narration, and subtitles.

The final edit combines generated clips, Amharic narration, English subtitles, and original background music.

The background score is mixed beneath the narration so that the spoken story remains clear.

## 6. Use of AI

AI tools were used to generate visual assets, moving clips, narration, and original music.

The production uses AI as a means of reconstructing and communicating a remembered place, not as evidence that the generated images are historical records.

The film must therefore be understood as an AI-generated creative interpretation rather than archival footage.

## 7. Design Trade-offs

The project prioritizes completing a coherent short documentary over achieving perfect historical reconstruction.

Because no historical photographs were available, the visuals are generated interpretations based on remembered descriptions.

The video-generation service also has a short clip-duration limitation, so the film is constructed from multiple short clips.

These constraints influenced the storyboard, shot composition, and editing approach.

## 8. Design Limitations

The generated visuals cannot guarantee exact historical accuracy. Details such as architecture, clothing, people, and surroundings may differ from the actual location.

The film communicates the memory and significance of BONO rather than claiming to reproduce its historical appearance exactly.
